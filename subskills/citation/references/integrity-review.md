# Citation Integrity Review

## Four Separate Conclusions

Answer these independently rather than merging them into one verified status:

1. Is the bibliographic structure valid: keys, required fields, duplicate entries, and actual citation scope?
2. Does the publication identity agree: title, authors, version, venue, year, volume/issue/pages, and DOI?
3. Has publication status changed: corrections, retractions, expressions of concern, and their relevance to this citation?
4. Does the source support the current sentence: research object, conditions, method, and conclusion?

The first three can be partly automated. The fourth requires reading the relevant source text. API hits, similar titles, citation counts, and venue quartiles cannot replace claim-support review.

## Full-Text Workflow

- Start with the exact sentence and identify the citation's role: background, definition, method, baseline, data/tool source, or substantive conclusion.
- Locate pages, sections, equations, figures, or experimental conditions in the source. Record the minimum necessary evidence and an accessible source. If only the abstract was read, state that evidence scope.
- Compare task objects, data, assumptions, comparison conditions, and claim strength. Distinguish direct source conclusions, the current paper's derivation, and general background.
- Label support as supported, partly supported, unsupported, or unverified. Do not base the result on reputation or reconstruct unread conclusions from memory.
- Proposals may relocate citations, split compound claims, or narrow their scope, subject to author approval. Do not replace sources or alter citation keys automatically.
- Read any publication-status notice. A correction does not necessarily invalidate a paper; a retracted work may legitimately be cited to discuss its retraction. Give the author the specific implications rather than silently removing it.

## Review Record

Use these fields for important claims. This is review material, not text to insert automatically into the manuscript.

| Field | Contents |
| --- | --- |
| Location and original sentence | File/paragraph and exact wording |
| Citation key and version | Existing key; published, preprint, or other version |
| Citation role | Background, method, baseline, data, tool, or conclusion |
| Verifiable source | Publisher page, DOI, full text, or authorized material |
| Evidence location | Page, section, equation, figure, or table |
| Evidence summary | Brief paraphrase rather than extended copying |
| Conditions and differences | Correspondence of objects, assumptions, data, and evidence strength |
| Publication status | Notices searched, notice found, or not checked; include the check date |
| Judgment | Supported / partly supported / unsupported / unverified |
| Proposal and approval | Intended changes and author approval |

## Quartiles and Sources

When using the citation skill's source preferences, record the JCR year and subject category. Do not silently choose the most favorable quartile for a multidisciplinary journal. Without an authoritative source or access, leave the quartile unverified; do not infer it from search snippets. A reference budget does not establish authenticity or relevance.
